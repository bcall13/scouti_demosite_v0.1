// Four Critical Failures

The Gap Between
Footage and Intelligence

The camera is the easy part. What breaks down is everything that comes after — the analysis, the interpretation, the feedback loop. These four failures compound into a development model that's leaving players, coaches, and families underserved.

Failure 01 — Time

The Extreme Time Deficit

Coaches simply do not have 12 hours on a Sunday to manually review match footage, clip highlights, and track the off-ball behaviors of 18 different players. The video piles up — unwatched and underutilized — while the coaching staff runs on instinct and memory instead.

"We filmed every match this season. I've watched maybe three of them."
01
Failure 02 — Objectivity

Subjective, Biased Evaluation

Without automated tracking, coaches are forced to rely on flawed human memory. They catch what's visible and miss what's subtle. The result is generic, subjective feedback — "you need to work harder" — rather than precise, tactical corrections grounded in objective truth. The best players get the most attention. Everyone else gets generalities.

"You need to work harder." — Every coach who doesn't have the data to say something better.
02
Failure 03 — Measurement

The Intangibles Go Unmeasured

Traditional stats only count discrete events — goals, passes, shots. They completely ignore the 89 minutes a player spends without the ball. Defensive rotations, off-ball support runs, spatial occupation, pressing shape — the behaviors that win or lose matches — are left entirely unquantified. They happen. Nobody captures them.

The most important moments in football are the ones that don't show up in the box score.
03
Failure 04 — Development Loop

The Development Dead End

Even when a coach manually identifies a tactical breakdown, there is no system to quickly translate that realization into a replicable week-over-week development plan or a specific Tuesday practice drill. Every insight dies in the film room. Nothing flows downstream into structured development, measurable progress, or a player profile that reflects growth over time.

Seeing the problem and fixing it are two completely different workflows. Right now, there's nothing connecting them.
04
// Who Pays the Price

The Downstream Cost

Four systemic failures produce three compounding consequences — felt by everyone in the ecosystem.

Impact — Players

Generic Feedback. Stunted Growth.

Players receive vague, subjective feedback that tells them what to feel but not what to fix. Without specific, objective data, individual development stalls — and the players with the most potential often go the most undercoached because their problems are subtle rather than obvious.

"Work harder, move faster, be more aggressive." No player has ever developed from that alone.
Impact — Parents

$3,000 a Year. No Proof of ROI.

Families are treating club soccer as a serious investment — financially and developmentally. They want objective proof their child is improving. What they get is a coach's memory and a gut feel. The gap between what parents are paying and what they're being shown is widening every season.

The question every DoC dreads: "Can you show me exactly how my kid has improved this year?"
Impact — Recruiting

A Highlight Reel Isn't a Profile.

Ambitious athletes are left relying on self-edited, highly biased highlight reels for college recruiting — because they have nothing else. No verified metrics. No objective performance history. No way to show a college coach the numbers behind the player. The recruiting process rewards presentation over performance.

A coach who can't develop isn't recruiting. They're curating. And the wrong players keep getting through.
